Chronicle of Sovereignty

Milestones of Swarajya (1630 – 1680)

From the sacred vow at Rohideshwar to the golden coronation at Raigad and naval expansion.

19 FEB 1630
19 FEB 1630

Birth at Fort Shivneri

Shivaji Maharaj was born to Jijau Mata and Shahaji Raje Bhosale in the triangular hill fortress of Shivneri, named after the goddess Shivai.

The Oath of Swarajya at Raireshwar

At the tender age of 15, Shivaji Maharaj and his Mawala companions cut their thumbs to offer a blood covenant before Bhagwan Shiva at Raireshwar Temple, dedicating their lives to establish *Hindavi Swarajya*.

1646
1646

Capture of Torna (Prachandgad)

First fort captured by Shivaji Maharaj without bloodshed from Adilshahi. Treasure unearthed during reconstruction at Torna directly funded the construction of the formidable Rajgad Fort.

10 NOV 1659
10 NOV 1659

Battle of Pratapgad & Slaying of Afzal Khan

In the dense jawli forests beneath Pratapgad, Shivaji Maharaj counteracted Afzal Khan's treacherous assassination attempt with the concealed Wagh Nakh (tiger claws) and Bichwa, decimating the Bijapuri host.

1666
1666

The Great Escape from Agra

Placed under house arrest by Aurangzeb at Agra, Shivaji Maharaj and young Prince Sambhaji executed an ingenious escape hidden inside sweet hampers, evading Mughal scouts across a 1,000-mile journey back to Maharashtra.

06 JUN 1674
06 JUN 1674

Grand Rajyabhisheka (Coronation) at Raigad

Consecrated as Chhatrapati by Gaga Bhatt atop Fort Raigad. The formal coronation asserted absolute Hindu sovereign authority, introducing the Raj Shaka era and royal gold 'Hon' coinage.

Tactical Supremacy

Ganimi Kava & Legendary Engagements

How light cavalry, intimate topographical knowledge, and sudden ambushes overwhelmed mammoth imperial armies.

Ambush at Umbrkhind (1661)

Kartalab Khan and Rai Bagan's 20,000 strong Mughal army was lured into a claustrophobic mountain pass in the Western Ghats. Without firing an open field volley, Maratha archers and horn-blowers from concealed cliffs forced total unconditional surrender.

Terrain As Weapon Zero Casualties

Battle of Salher (1672)

The largest open-field pitch battle between the Marathas and the Mughal Empire. Near the highest fort in Maharashtra, Maratha cavalry under Prataprao Gujar routed the combined armies of Ikhlas Khan and Bahlol Khan in open plains.

Open Cavalry Charge 100,000+ Forces

Naval Ascendancy & Padmadurg

To break the Siddis of Janjira, the Portuguese, and English dominance of Konkan trade, Shivaji Maharaj built Sindhudurg, Suvarnadurg, and Padmadurg, creating India's first indigenously organized navy of Gurabs and Galbats.

Father of Indian Navy Arvi / Konkan Sea
Engineering of the Sahyadri

Maratha Fort Architecture

The geometric and strategic marvels described in the contemporary treaty Ramchandra Pant Amatya's Adnyapatra: "Forts are the very essence of Swarajya."

Gomukhi Darwaja (Cow-Mouth Gate)

Main gates were intentionally constructed between two overlapping bastion bastions with a 90-degree curved blind entrance. Artillery and battering rams could never gain direct straight-line momentum.

Natural Rainwater Harvesting Cisterns

Perched atop rock massifs without perennial rivers, forts featured carved rock water tanks (*Tanks of Ganga-Jamuna*). Shivaji Maharaj decreed: "A fort must have water to sustain garrisons through two full summers."

Lead-Molten Marine Foundations

At Sindhudurg, hundreds of metric tons of molten lead and iron were poured into bedrock fissures under roaring sea waters to anchor underwater foundation stones against fierce Arabian Sea monsoons.

The Adnyapatra Testament

Curatorial Mission

About Swarajya Atlas & IGMIRAL

SWARAJYA ATLAS is an interactive digital preservation initiative presented by IGMIRAL. Our charter is to bring the timeless fortification architecture, coastal dockyards, and military discipline of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj to researchers, students, and travelers globally through high-fidelity cartography and historically rigorous documentation.

Every fort in this atlas represents more than basalt masonry; it symbolizes the collective vow of social justice, religious liberty, and unyielding self-governance (*Hindavi Swarajya*).
